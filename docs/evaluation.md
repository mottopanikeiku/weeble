# Evaluation

This is the protocol, not a results page. No measurements are implied by the defaults below. I use policy seed 0 for demos, chosen before looking at results.

## Policies and inference

I evaluate `go1-walk`, `go1-walk-push`, `go1-getup`, and `g1-walk`, each trained with seeds 0, 1, and 2. The Go1 walking arms differ only in upstream `pert_config.enable`: false for the baseline, true for the push-trained arm. Training uses the pinned Playground defaults and Warp backend; evaluation does not retrain policies. Inference uses the actor mean, with observation noise, domain randomization, and automatic training disturbances disabled. Reset clears policy observation/action history. Physics, gains, action scaling, clipping, and observation timing come from the policy export, not a new controller.

## Velocity tracking

Each walking policy runs the Cartesian product of three evenly spaced values per command axis. Go1 ranges are forward ±1.5 m/s, lateral ±0.8 m/s, yaw ±1.2 rad/s. G1 ranges are forward ±1 m/s, lateral ±0.5 m/s, yaw ±1 rad/s. These stay within the trained ranges, checked against each export. Each episode lasts 10 s from the nominal pose, with the first 2 s discarded. Tracking continues after a physical fall rather than removing its subsequent errors. I report mean absolute error separately for body-frame forward velocity, lateral velocity and yaw rate: `forward_mae`, `lateral_mae` (m/s) and `yaw_mae` (rad/s). Only numerical failures terminate early; their available-sample errors are labelled conditional, and the completed-episode fraction includes those failures.

The measurement frame is the named trunk/pelvis body frame (`mjOBJ_XBODY`), not the body's principal-inertia frame (`mjOBJ_BODY`). Those axes differ substantially for Go1. The first evaluation invocations used the latter by mistake; their raw trials are retained with `superseded` manifests and the full trials are rerun after this correction. A known freejoint-velocity test checks this coordinate convention.

## Cross-terrain walking

I fix this protocol before running the comparison. The arms are default `Go1JoystickFlatTerrain` (`go1-walk`) and default `Go1JoystickRoughTerrain` (`go1-walk-rough`), each with training seeds 0, 1 and 2. Both upstream defaults disable perturbations. The demo uses rough seed 0, regardless of evaluation results. The two evaluation scenes are the exact flat and rough training scenes; I change only the selected scene, not either actor, gains, robot mechanism or physics options.

The first rough-task smoke run, [`rough-smoke-v2`](../results/runs/rough-smoke-v2/), exposed constraint-buffer overflow with the upstream `njmax=60`. I retain it as a superseded smoke run, not a valid trained policy. Subsequent rough runs raise only that allocation to `njmax=256`; the reward, PPO settings, robot, terrain and solver options remain the upstream defaults, and overflow warnings stay enabled. The effective configuration is recorded in every run and export.

Each arm/seed runs both scenes with the Cartesian product of forward commands {-0.5, 0, 0.5} m/s, lateral {-0.3, 0, 0.3} m/s, and yaw {-0.6, 0, 0.6} rad/s: 27 commands per cell. These lower speeds keep the ten-second trajectories inside the rough scene's 20 m square. Every trial starts at x=y=0, base z=0.35 m, identity orientation, nominal joint angles and zero velocities, with empty controller history. I discard the first 2 s for tracking and measure the remaining 8 s in the body frame. The common starting height is the rough task's nominal pose; it also avoids starting either policy inside the terrain.

I record a fall if tilt exceeds 60° or any enabled non-foot geometry touches the floor, checked at every integration step, including the discarded interval. Physical falls do not stop tracking; numerical failures do, and remain failures with their partial-sample errors labelled. There are no shoves or browser props, noise or domain randomization. I report forward/lateral/yaw mean absolute error and the fraction of episodes with a fall, per seed and pooled equally across seeds. This tests this one heightfield, not unseen terrain generalization. Raw trials live in `results/terrain/`; the normal report command writes `terrain.csv` and `terrain_seeds.csv`.

## Experimental humanoid get-up

The custom `G1Getup` task uses the full-contact fall scene, nominal joints and uniform SO(3) orientations dropped from 1.0 m, held for 1 s before a six-second recovery episode. Its actor has no commands or gait clock. Dense rewards favour torso-up alignment and standing pelvis height, with joint-limit, action-rate and torque penalties. This is a new task, not a Playground humanoid recovery policy.

The initial custom configuration copies Go1Getup's PPO settings except `num_resets_per_eval=4`, selected before any G1 training results. At its requested 50M steps this gives an eight-second explicit reset interval rather than cutting the six-second recovery window short, as the earlier dog diagnosis showed. The model's physics options are unchanged; full-body contacts use `naconmax=64*8192` and `njmax=512` allocation. Candidate exports remain under their training-run directory, not in the demo policy directory, until the standard-MuJoCo drop test qualifies them. The report command writes every attempted candidate's fallen/already-upright/all counts to `g1_getup.csv`.

Before training I fix evaluation at 200 drops using the dog's orientation distribution, zero velocities, nominal joints, one-second settling, fresh controller reset and six-second policy window. The humanoid's size changes only drop height to 1.0 m and standing pelvis height to 0.65 m. The other success rules are unchanged: tilt below 15°, linear speed below 0.2 m/s and angular speed below 0.5 rad/s continuously for 1 s at physics-step resolution. I separate already-upright from fallen starts. A demo requires at least 80% success among fallen starts; otherwise the failed experiment remains code and results only. Seed 0 of the final stated configuration is the only demo candidate. I cap this experiment's cloud jobs at $5 and disclose each attempted configuration rather than replacing poor seeds.

The unchanged initial configuration completed as [`g1-getup-s0-t50m-v2`](../results/runs/g1-getup-s0-t50m-v2/). It recovered from none of the tested fallen starts and was not promoted. The candidate export stays under that run, not `policies/`; [`g1_getup.csv`](../results/report/g1_getup.csv) regenerates its counts from the [raw drop trials](../results/getup-diagnostic/20261014T184451Z-87018d1a/).

After that run, its [training curve](../results/runs/g1-getup-s0-t50m-v2/curve.csv) still showed rising pelvis-height reward and falling penalties in the last evaluation. I therefore choose one final configuration before running it: 300M requested steps, otherwise the same task, rewards, physics and PPO settings, including four explicit resets per evaluation. The schedule now allows 46 s between explicit resets, longer than the unchanged six-second episode; this budget change is not isolated from reset frequency. I train seed 0 from scratch and apply the same predeclared drop protocol and promotion threshold. Both configurations remain in the report, within the same $5 experiment cap. A failed final run will not be extended or replaced with another seed.

The final run, [`g1-getup-s0-t300m-v2`](../results/runs/g1-getup-s0-t300m-v2/), completed from the clean source commit that declared this choice. Its environment configuration matches the initial run; the only PPO configuration difference is the requested timestep count. It again recovered from none of the [tested fallen starts](../results/getup-diagnostic/20261014T202800Z-cff38d9a/), despite a higher training reward. I stopped the experiment. Neither candidate is a demo policy; both exports, curves, costs and raw outcomes remain available, with counts regenerated in [`g1_getup.csv`](../results/report/g1_getup.csv).

## Push recovery

Both Go1 walking arms and G1 receive a fixed forward command of 0.5 m/s. Each trial starts at the nominal pose and walks for a uniformly sampled 2–3 s before the push, so trials cover different gait phases. A horizontal world-frame force acts on Go1 `trunk` or G1 `pelvis` for exactly 0.1 s. Its direction is uniform on [0, 2π), and its magnitude is J / 0.1, where J is impulse in N s. Each trial continues for 3 s after the force ends. I sample at least 100 independent directions/onset times per trained seed and impulse, including J=0 controls. Evaluation trial seeds are fixed in the config; identical Go1 trial indices have identical directions and onset times across impulses, both training arms, and C MuJoCo/MJX.

The Go1 grid is 0, 5, …, 50 N s. The G1 grid is 0, 10, 20, 30, 40, 60, 80, 100 N s. Failure is base tilt strictly greater than 60° or any non-foot robot geometry contacting the floor at any integration step, including before the push. Non-finite state, observation or action, simulator errors, and MuJoCo numerical warnings are failures, not retries. Success means no failure through the whole trial. Contact detection uses the actual exported collision model. The walking assets have feet-only floor collisions: absent torso/limb collision geometry cannot produce a contact, so the tilt criterion does most of the work. I do not add collision geometry just for evaluation. Every manifest records which floor-contact geometry is enabled.

## Get-up

For each Go1 get-up seed I sample at least 200 trials. The starting base orientation is uniform on SO(3), generated by normalizing four independent standard normal samples. The base starts at z=0.5 m with zero velocity and nominal joint angles. It drops and settles for 1 s under joint position control holding those initial angles, without policy actions. The resulting physical state is passed through a fresh policy reset, then the policy runs for 6 s. This isolates orientation recovery rather than the upstream mixture of nominal/random poses and random joints.

Success requires tilt strictly below 15°, trunk height at least 0.2 m, base linear speed below 0.2 m/s, and base angular speed below 0.5 rad/s continuously for 1 s, sampled at every physics integration step. The height condition excludes lying flat on the belly. Tilt and height use the integrated freejoint pose without recomputing the actor's sensor stage. I report success fraction and median time from policy start to the start of the first qualifying one-second interval. The policy still runs the full 6 s; numerical failure later makes the trial a failure. Contact with the floor is allowed during get-up. Raw rows retain the initial orientation, settled pose, and recovery time.

The headline counts only trials whose saved `settled_qpos` is fallen when the policy starts. A pose is already upright if its integrated freejoint tilt is below 15° and its height is at least 0.2 m; speed is not a pose criterion. These are the same orientation/height tests used for recovery. `getup.csv` separates `fallen`, `already_upright` and `all` starts, per seed. Missing or invalid settled poses are labelled `unclassified`, never silently counted as fallen. This classification uses the recorded states, not a new drop distribution or a changed success rule. Recovery-time medians are conditional on success within the named group.

In `getup.csv`, `equal_seed_success` is the mean of the per-seed success fractions; `count_success` is total successes divided by total trials. They agree for individual-seed rows, but can differ when the settled-pose groups contain different numbers of trials per seed. `ci_low` and `ci_high` describe the equal-seed estimate. The README gives per-seed counts rather than an unnamed pooled percentage. [getup_trials.csv](../results/report/getup_trials.csv) records the classification of every trial.

### Get-up diagnosis

The initial 50-million-step seed 0 recovered in 35 of 200 C MuJoCo drops ([raw trials](../results/eval/20261014T085129Z-c3c5e09b/)). Its final two [training evaluations](../results/runs/go1-getup-s0/curve.csv) rose from 6.265 to 7.036 reward rather than establishing a plateau. Before changing evaluation thresholds, I compare the same drop protocol in training-matched Warp and separately measure the upstream reset distribution in both backends. Each diagnostic requests 200 trials and the unchanged six-second policy window and one-second standing criterion; raw files live in `results/getup-diagnostic/`.

Upstream reset samples a random-orientation, random-joint pose at 0.5 m with probability 0.6, otherwise the nominal home pose. Root linear and angular velocities are uniform on [-0.5, 0.5], and joint-position control holds the sampled angles while settling for 0.5 s. This differs from the original evaluation's all-random orientations, nominal joints, zero velocity and 1 s settling; it is not simply an easier/harder ordering. Upside-down orientations are included during training. Both evaluation distributions use a 300-control-step (6 s) policy window. The upstream-distribution diagnostic retains the cached sensor stage after settling, as upstream reset does.

On the upstream reset distribution, C MuJoCo succeeds in 97/200 trials: 83/84 nominal starts but only 14/116 dropped starts ([raw](../results/getup-diagnostic/20261014T093628Z-eb0c6fa9/), [regenerated comparison](../results/report/getup_diagnostic.csv)). Staying upright from a nominal start is not evidence of getting back up.

The environment's episode limit is 6 s, but Brax also explicitly resets training worlds between optimization epochs. Under the 50M-step defaults, its batch size, evaluation count and ten resets per evaluation imply only 160 control steps (3.2 s) between those resets. The [`training.csv`](../results/report/training.csv) report computes this interval from each run's actual configuration. Increasing the requested timestep budget also lengthens that interval; it is not an isolated change to update count.

The final get-up configuration requests 200 million steps, with all other upstream parameters unchanged. This lengthens Brax's explicit reset interval from 3.2 to 12.4 s, allowing the full six-second recovery episode rather than resetting training worlds partway through it. I selected it after the rising 50M curve and poor recovery from dropped training-distribution starts, not by selecting a seed. Seed 0 of this final configuration is the demo policy. I evaluated its 200 random-drop trials in C MuJoCo before training seeds 1 and 2. The unchanged random-drop protocol supplies the headline result, separately from already-upright starts. All final-config seeds remain in the results, including the weak seed 2; there is no selective extension or replacement of a poor seed.

The first Warp training-reset diagnostic reached 182 trials before its time limit: 79/79 nominal starts and 15/103 dropped starts succeeded. These incomplete observations are retained and labelled, not treated as the planned 200-trial measurement. The original-drop Warp invocation was preempted before recording a trial. `getup-diagnostic --initialization drop` selects the original protocol; `upstream` selects the training reset distribution.

`--remote-run <training-run-id>` evaluates that run's stored policy from the Volume, even after a newer configuration replaces the browser export. The policy hash and original training-run ID remain in the evaluation manifest. `fetch-getup-diagnostic --remote-run <diagnostic-launch-id>` retrieves persisted diagnostic trials without repeating them.

For the interrupted G1 run, `export-checkpoint --remote-run g1-walk-s0 --checkpoint-steps 181043200` exports the last persisted checkpoint without further optimization. Its manifest links the original full configuration and curve. `check-policy --policy g1-walk-s0 --gpu none` runs the fixed 27-command C-MuJoCo tracking grid before accepting it for the demo; these checks live in `results/policy-checks/`, separate from the final full evaluation.

G1 seeds 1 and 2 retain the default 200M-step configuration and each has a 60-minute L4 time limit. Seed 0 is not retrained or replaced. The three-seed results disclose its shorter completed training budget alongside the full runs in `training.csv`; G1 is not a same-duration seed comparison.

The same report command writes `getup_diagnostic.csv`, with the original/revised training-run IDs, both initialization distributions, backend, counts, success rate, median recovery time, and trial-bootstrap intervals for the single tested seed. Historical results kept for this comparison are labelled in their manifests; they are not pooled with the final configuration.

## Go1 ablation and uncertainty

All three training seeds in each arm are required for the complete ablation. At each impulse, I pool equal numbers of trials per seed. I fit a non-increasing survival curve with weighted isotonic regression, then linearly interpolate the first 50% survival crossing to obtain J50. A curve above 50% at the largest tested impulse is right-censored; one below 50% at zero is left-censored. I report the grid bound, not an extrapolated threshold. A value exactly at 50% is an observed crossing.

I also report each seed's survival curve and J50 separately. The arm comparison uses the equal-seed pooled curves, not a selected seed.

The report uses 2,000 hierarchical bootstrap replicates. It resamples training seeds, then whole trial indices within each sampled seed. One index carries the same direction and onset time through every impulse in the grid, so all those outcomes are resampled together. The Go1 arm comparison also uses the same sampled indices in both arms. Percentile intervals are 95%; J50 intervals are omitted if any replicate is censored, with the censored fraction and grid bounds reported instead. Incomplete trial-index grids do not get a joint bootstrap interval. With fewer than three complete seeds the output is labelled partial. An earlier report sampled impulse cells independently; its unchanged tables and figures are preserved in [the superseded report archive](../results/superseded/independent-impulse-report.zip).

## Sim-to-sim check

The optional `mjx` runner uses the same exported model, deterministic actor, observation timing, command grid, push directions, onset times, and trial IDs as C MuJoCo. It runs the actual training physics backend, MJX Warp, on a Modal CUDA GPU; the export must have `training_config.impl='warp'`. Model parameters, integration settings, and contact/constraint capacities come from that export and its training config. Reset uses Warp forward dynamics; a compiled scan checks numerical state, base tilt, active Warp contacts, and capacity overflow at every physics substep before copying the final control-step state into the policy runtime. Only trained seed 0 is evaluated. Tracking/push comparisons use the standard protocol; the additional get-up diagnosis uses both explicitly named reset distributions above.

The final budget-limited comparison uses only Go1 walking seed 0: the unchanged 27-command tracking grid and 20 trials per impulse, rather than 100. `train/launch.py gap --policy go1-walk-s0 --backend mjx --gpu L4 --minutes 40` selects it. Directions, onset times, force durations, episode windows and failure rules are unchanged. The report pairs these with the same first 20 C-MuJoCo trial IDs at each impulse; its gap J50 uses those paired C trials, not the larger three-seed C estimate. This is a small one-seed comparison.

Warp model preparation uses Playground's `mjx_env.put_model`, including its default warning options. Only arrays needed by the actor and tracking metrics cross back to NumPy; contacts and capacity checks stay on the GPU. A GPU parity check compares this small state transfer against MuJoCo's full `get_data_into` conversion for all copied arrays, observations, and body velocities. The initial GPU invocation timed out while copying full contact buffers and emitting iteration-limit warnings that training does not enable; its partial trials are retained and excluded.

Single-world Warp evaluation does not allocate the contact pool sized for 8,192 training worlds. For these rigid models, it uses the smaller of that pool and a conservative upper bound: MuJoCo's `mjMAXCONPAIR` times every mask-enabled or explicit geom pair, including excluded and same-body pairs in the bound. This changes memory allocation, not contact filtering, solver parameters or the protocol. Overflow is still checked every physics substep. GPU parity also compares a control step against the original full-capacity allocation.

Each parity invocation uses its own Volume directory, so concurrent policy checks do not share report files. The sensor-stage test copies native data before Warp conversion, which otherwise calls kinematics in place, and restores the original cached site matrices for the identical-field comparison.

## Running and reporting

Standard C MuJoCo evaluation runs on Modal CPU; the MJX Warp comparison runs on Modal GPU. `python train/launch.py eval --policy go1-walk-s0 --gpu none --minutes 20` launches CPU evaluation directly through `modal run`. `--minutes` sets the remote function timeout. The launcher records source revision, dirty status, launch configuration, complete wall time and a compute-cost upper bound using [Modal list prices](https://modal.com/pricing), including CPU and memory with a 10% margin. It needs a configured Modal account, not a separate spending tool. The commands below are equivalent container entrypoints. CPU containers set the numerical libraries to one thread; no local simulation is required.

```sh
python -m eval.run --root . --output results/c-mujoco
python -m eval.run --root . --output results/mjx --backend mjx --seeds 0 --provenance evaluation-provenance.json
python -m eval.report results/c-mujoco results/mjx --output results/report
```

`run_suite(root, output, backend='mujoco', seed_filter=None, *, config=None, policy_filter=None, modal_app_id=None, provenance=None, getup_initialization='drop')` is the programmatic entrypoint and returns the new run directory. The Modal trainer passes `provenance={'git_sha': ..., 'git_dirty': ..., 'modal_app_id': ..., 'gpu': ...}` so the container does not need a git checkout. `gpu` is the selected GPU type for MJX Warp and `none` for CPU evaluation; the GPU route requires it. The CLI accepts the same dictionary from `--provenance path.json`. `policy_filter` accepts a policy stem or an iterable of stems. `SuiteConfig` exposes grids, trial counts, thresholds, durations, trial random seed, and suite selection. `--policy go1-walk-s0` (repeatable) permits early per-policy downloads. Use the `getup-diagnostic` stage for training-reset comparisons, so those separate results cannot be pooled into the standard protocol.

Each invocation creates a separate run directory with a summary `manifest.json`. Each `<backend>/<policy>/` subdirectory holds its own `manifest.json`, append-only `trials.jsonl`, and matching `trials.csv`; those files can be downloaded independently. A flush after each row makes completed trials downloadable while a run is in progress. Manifests include git SHA/dirty status, full effective config, selected policies and trained seeds, policy/model SHA256, package versions, GPU type from provenance (`none` on CPU), backend, timing, Modal app ID, completion status, and collision-model limitations. The selector and raw row backend remain `mjx`; manifests record `backend_detail='mjx-warp'` to name the actual physics implementation. Actor weights are referenced by checkpoint hash rather than duplicated in the manifest. The runner never overwrites an earlier run. Set `modal_app_id` or provide it in `provenance` from the trainer when available; otherwise the environment value is recorded, or null if unavailable. Trial failures retain error text; non-finite diagnostic numbers are written as null. Policy manifests update every 100 rows and at completion. A hard process interruption can leave status `running`; caught interruptions use `interrupted`. Completed raw rows remain usable.

The Modal wrapper compresses raw JSONL and CSV after each invocation finishes, before downloading them. `python -m eval.report results/eval --output results/report --update-readme` regenerates the evaluation tables and figures, training costs and GPU benchmarks. Guarded job charges come from the committed `results/spend.jsonl`; standalone launches contribute their separate list-price cost manifests. Matching run IDs are reconciled rather than charged twice.

Benchmark tables distinguish the steady-state cost per million environment control steps from the full invocation's ledger charge divided by its measured steps; the latter includes compilation, warmup, and download time. These zero-action physics measurements are not PPO training-speed estimates.

If a download fails after the Volume commit, `python train/launch.py fetch-eval --gpu none --minutes 2 --remote-run <evaluation-directory-or-launch-id> --run-id <download-id>` retrieves the original manifest and trials without re-running or changing the measurements. The original invocation remains marked failed in the spend ledger; the recovered evaluation manifest records whether simulation itself completed. Failed invocation wrappers capture the source revision at launch, not at download completion.

For interrupted training, `fetch-training --remote-run <training-run-id>` retrieves the last persisted source manifest, curve and checkpoint inventory. It does not turn an incomplete run into a completed policy or overwrite its billing manifest. Large original PPO parameter tuples are on the Volume and the [checkpoint release](https://github.com/mottopanikeiku/pushable/releases/tag/policies-2026-10-14); `python -m train.fetch_checkpoints` downloads and verifies them for the parity tests.

The report command consumes raw JSONL (also `.jsonl.gz`) and their manifests, rejects duplicate trial IDs rather than silently counting reruns twice, and writes every generated table, SVG figure, and `summary.json` into the chosen output directory. It includes input hashes and bootstrap settings. Commit the raw trials and manifests used for published numbers; the report does not invent missing seeds or replace failed trials. Results marked `superseded` in their manifest are excluded. The one command above regenerates the report's tracking, push, get-up, J50, ablation, and one-seed backend-gap tables and figures. Bootstrap intervals describe the tested seeds/trials, not all training runs or hardware.

The report also reads `results/terrain/` and `results/getup-diagnostic/`, and writes `web/src/data/survival.json` for browser overlays. Each demo walking entry contains the measured impulse grid, survived/total counts, pooled seed IDs, survival fractions and raw source paths. It does not interpolate between impulses or invent push results for the rough-ground policy.

Raw `error` strings include expected `TrialFailure` exceptions for outcomes such as `no_recovery`. Those are failed trials, not software defects; `failure=simulator_error` denotes an unexpected exception.

The same command updates the README's marked headline, results and cost blocks, and the get-up count in its Limits section, from these tables and manifests. Incomplete ablation/get-up/G1/gap inputs raise an error rather than replacing published measurements with partial results. Interrupted training used for a published checkpoint is explicitly labelled in `training.csv`; its full original invocation charge is retained. `invocations.csv` lists every cloud job, including failures, distinguishes historical charges/bookings from later list-price estimates, and links each available run manifest.

`--update-readme` explicitly requests publication into the README. Omit it for partial analyses or alternate report directories; ordinary report generation does not edit the README.
